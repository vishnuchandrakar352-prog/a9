Proportion & the Rule of Thirds

Why does tucking in a shirt make an outfit look better? It is not magic, it is proportion. A few simple ideas from art and design explain most of it.

01

The rule of thirds

The eye finds a split of one third to two thirds more pleasing than an exact half. In clothing, that means a short top over long trousers or skirt, or a long top over shorter legs, usually looks better than cutting the body in two equal parts.

02

Where the waist sits

Your visual waist is wherever the eye sees a break.

  • A tuck or belt at the natural waist creates a 1:2 split.
  • High-waisted trousers lengthen the legs.
  • An untucked hip-length top shortens them.

03

Hemlines guide the eye

The eye stops where a hem ends, so hems work best at a narrow point: just above or below the knee, at mid-calf where the leg narrows, or at the ankle. A hem across the widest part of the calf can make legs look heavier.

04

Volume and balance

If one area has volume, keep another area streamlined. A full skirt pairs with a fitted top; wide trousers with a neater top; an oversized jumper with slim trousers. Balance makes even dramatic shapes look effortless.

05

Colour as line

Matching colours blur breaks and lengthen; contrast creates them. A monochrome outfit reads as one long column. A bright belt draws attention to the waist. Use colour to place the breaks exactly where you want them.
